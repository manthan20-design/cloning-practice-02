The American dollar, also known as the United States Dollar (USD), is the official currency of the United States of America. Its symbol is $, and one dollar is equal to 100 cents. The US dollar is one of the most widely used currencies in the world and plays an important role in international trade, business, and finance. Common dollar banknotes include $1, $2, $5, $10, $20, $50, and $100. The one-dollar bill features George Washington, the first President of the United States, while the hundred-dollar bill features Benjamin Franklin, one of America's Founding Fathers. American banknotes are made from a blend of cotton and linen rather than ordinary paper. The back of the one-dollar bill displays a pyramid and an eagle, which are part of the Great Seal of the United States. The US dollar became the official monetary unit of the country in 1792. Many countries keep US dollars in their foreign exchange reserves, and the currency is widely used in global trade. The exchange rate between the American dollar and other currencies, such as the Indian rupee, changes according to market conditions, interest rates, inflation, and economic developments.
